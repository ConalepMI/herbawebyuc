Inside a Water Well Build: 12 Villages, One Summer

Our field coordinator followed the drilling crew from site survey to first flow — here's what it actually takes to turn a donation into a working well, mile by mile.

Aria Chen
Field Coordinator
PublishedAugust 4, 2026
Read Time6 minutes
Site 7 of 12, Day 3 — the drilling crew breaks through to the water table.

The village of Amboko hasn't had a reliable water source in six years. Every morning before sunrise, women and children walk two hours to the nearest river, fill jerry cans, and walk two hours back — a routine that eats the better part of a school day and a workday alike. This summer, that changed, and we followed the entire process from the first soil sample to the first turn of the hand pump.

Hopewell's clean water campaign funds well construction across twelve villages in this region, working through Clearwater Rural Alliance, one of our longest-standing verified NGO partners. Before a single donor dollar reaches the ground, the campaign goes through the same manifest process every Hopewell cause follows: a documented needs assessment, a costed build plan, and a disbursement schedule tied to verified milestones.

The Survey: Where a Well Actually Gets Decided

Well placement isn't guesswork. A hydrogeologist surveys groundwater depth, tests for contamination risk, and checks the site against seasonal flood maps — a well placed carelessly can run dry within two years or become unusable during rainy season. In Amboko, three candidate sites were tested before the crew found a spot with a stable water table at 42 meters.

Field survey, Day 1 — testing groundwater depth ahead of drilling.

Twelve Days, Start to Flow

Once a site is approved, the build itself moves fast. A typical well in this program takes twelve working days: drilling, casing, a concrete apron to prevent surface contamination, and installation of a hand pump rated for heavy daily use. Local labor is hired wherever possible — six of the eight crew members on this build were from Amboko or a neighboring village.

  • Days 1–2: Site prep and drilling rig setup.
  • Days 3–7: Drilling and casing to the water table.
  • Days 8–10: Concrete apron and drainage construction.
  • Days 11–12: Pump installation and water-quality testing.

"The first time the pump ran clean, the whole village came out. Kids were filling bottles just to watch it happen again."

— Kwame Boateng, Clearwater Rural Alliance site lead

Verifying the Result

Every completed well is water-tested for bacterial contamination and mineral content before it's marked "delivered" on the campaign dashboard, and photographed for the donor updates tied to that specific campaign. This is the same disbursement record that shows up in your account if you gave to this cause — the well in the photo above is one of the twelve your donation helped fund.

Amboko, Day 14 — the first full morning of clean water access.

What's Next for the Other Eleven

Four more wells are complete as of this writing, with the remaining seven scheduled through the rest of the year, weather permitting. Each will follow the identical survey-build-verify process — and each will get its own dated update on the campaign page, so donors can see exactly which village their gift reached.

If you'd like to follow along, the Wells for 12 Villages campaign updates weekly with new disbursement records as each site is completed.
