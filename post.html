Twelve wells, one dry season, and the villages that didn't wait for rain

Priya Nair
Field Program Lead
Jul 28, 2026 6 min read 14 comments

In March, the well in Kothapalli ran to mud by mid-morning. Women were walking four kilometers before sunrise to beat the queue at the next village's hand pump — a routine repeated across twelve districts where the Wells for 12 Villages campaign had promised relief, but hadn't yet broken ground.

Four months later, the campaign has reached 55% of its funding goal and completed survey work in all twelve target villages. This is the story of what actually happens between a donation and a working well — the part of the process most impact reports skip.

Step one: the survey nobody sees

Before a single rupee reaches a construction crew, a hydrogeology team spends two to three weeks per village testing water tables, checking seasonal rainfall records, and mapping walking distances from existing water sources. It's slow, unglamorous work, and it's also the single biggest factor in whether a well still produces water five years from now.

"We've seen wells fail in eighteen months because someone skipped the water table survey to save two weeks. We don't skip it — ever."

Of the twelve villages surveyed this season, two were flagged as poor sites and redirected to a rainwater harvesting design instead. That's not a delay we report as a setback — it's the audit process working exactly as intended.

Step two: construction, in the open

Every well site gets a visible signboard listing the funding source, expected completion date, and a phone number for community feedback. Local contractors are paid in three installments — on siting, on drilling completion, and on final pump installation — each one verified by an independent field auditor before release.

By the end of the dry season, we expect all twelve sites to have working infrastructure. Three will still be short of full funding — which is where this campaign, and the donors reading this, come in.

What your donation actually funds

  • Survey & siting — hydrogeology assessment and community consultation
  • Drilling & casing — borehole drilling to verified depth, with steel casing
  • Pump & platform — hand pump installation and a concrete apron to prevent contamination
  • 12-month maintenance — a local caretaker stipend and spare parts fund

None of this happens without a first gift. If you'd like to see exactly where the remaining balance goes, the full financial breakdown for this campaign is updated weekly on the causes page.
